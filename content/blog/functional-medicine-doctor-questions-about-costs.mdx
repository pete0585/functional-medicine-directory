## Start With the Money Conversation, Not After It

Choosing a Functional Medicine Doctor provider is partly a clinical decision and partly a practical one. Even when a provider seems like a strong fit for your goals, unclear costs can create stress, delay care, or lead to an unpleasant surprise months later. The good news is that most of the uncertainty can be reduced by asking a focused set of questions before you commit.

This guide is a decision aid, not a clinical protocol or individualized legal or financial advice. It does not quote prices, because fees vary by provider, location, and service mix. It also does not promise that any service will be covered by insurance. Instead, it gives you a structured way to gather information so you can compare providers on equal footing and decide what works for your situation. You should discuss your specific circumstances with a qualified professional, including your insurance plan administrator and the provider's billing staff.

## Why Cost Questions Belong in Provider Selection

Cost is not a side issue in Functional Medicine provider selection. The way a practice structures visits, testing, and follow-up shapes both your total spending and your ability to stay consistent with a plan. Two providers may look similar on a website but differ in how they bill, what they include in a visit, and how often they expect to see you.

Asking about costs early also helps you:

- Compare offers using the same criteria rather than reacting to a single number.
- Understand which parts are likely to be paid out of pocket.
- Identify questions you need to bring to your insurer.
- Plan for follow-up, which is often where ongoing expenses accumulate.

For general guidance on selecting a doctor or health care service, the U.S. National Library of Medicine's MedlinePlus resource [Choosing a Doctor or Health Care Service](https://medlineplus.gov/choosingadoctororhealthcareservice.html) offers a useful starting point. It covers broad considerations for choosing a clinician; it is not specific to Functional Medicine billing, so treat it as a companion resource rather than a complete answer.

## A Checklist of Billing and Insurance Questions

Use the questions below as a script. You can ask them by phone, in a portal message, or at the start of a consultation. Write down the answers so you can compare providers later.

### 1. How is the practice structured for payment?

- Does the practice bill insurance directly, or is it a self-pay (cash-pay) practice?
- If it is self-pay, are there any options such as superbills or receipts I can submit to my insurer myself?
- Are there membership or subscription models? If so, what is included and what is billed separately?
- Are there any administrative fees, new-patient fees, or records fees I should expect?

### 2. What does a standard visit include?

- How long is an initial visit, and how long are follow-up visits?
- Is the visit fee all-inclusive, or are there separate charges for things like extended counseling, care coordination, or messaging?
- Are there charges for messages, portal questions, or phone calls outside of visits?
- What happens if a visit runs longer than scheduled?

### 3. How are laboratory tests and other diagnostics handled?

- Which labs does the provider typically order, and where are they sent?
- Will the lab bill me directly, or will the practice bill me and then the lab separately?
- Are there tests that are usually not covered by insurance?
- Can I get an estimate or a range before tests are ordered?
- If a test is recommended, can we discuss alternatives, including waiting or using a different approach?

You do not need to decide in advance which tests you will accept. The goal is to understand the process, the likely billing path, and who to contact if a bill is unclear.

### 4. What should I ask my insurance plan?

- Is this provider in-network or out-of-network for my plan?
- What is my deductible, and how much of it remains?
- What is my coinsurance or copay for an office visit with this type of provider?
- Are there separate rules for laboratory services, and does the lab need to be in-network?
- Do I need a referral or prior authorization for any part of this care?
- If the provider is out-of-network, what portion, if any, might be reimbursed?

Insurance rules vary widely, and coverage decisions are made by your plan, not by the provider's office. Confirm details directly with your insurer and get reference numbers for your calls when possible.

### 5. What about follow-up and ongoing support?

- How often does the provider typically schedule follow-up visits?
- Are follow-ups included in a package, or billed per visit?
- What is the policy for rescheduling, cancellations, or no-shows?
- Is there a charge for between-visit support, such as portal messages or care coordination?
- If my plan changes or I lose coverage, how does that affect ongoing care?

Follow-up is often where cost questions matter most, because a single initial visit is rarely the whole picture. Ask about the expected rhythm of care so you can estimate your own budget over time.

### 6. What happens if I need to pause or stop?

- Is there a minimum commitment or contract?
- Can I pause or cancel, and are there any fees for doing so?
- Will I still have access to my records?
- Who do I contact if I have a billing dispute?

These questions are practical, not adversarial. A provider who is comfortable answering them is often easier to work with over the long term.

## How to Compare Providers Without Guessing

Once you have answers from two or three providers, put them side by side. A simple table can help:

| Question | Provider A | Provider B | Provider C |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Billing model (insurance vs. self-pay) | | | |
| Initial visit length and fee structure | | | |
| Follow-up frequency and billing | | | |
| Lab billing path | | | |
| Cancellation and rescheduling policy | | | |
| Between-visit support charges | | | |
| Contact for billing questions | | | |

When you fill this in, avoid treating any single answer as decisive. A provider with a higher visit fee might include more time or coordination, while a lower fee might come with more separate charges. The point is to see the whole picture rather than one line item.

It can also help to separate questions into three buckets:

1. **Certain costs** — fees the practice can state clearly, such as visit charges or membership fees.
2. **Variable costs** — amounts that depend on what is ordered, such as labs or imaging.
3. **Unknowns** — items that depend on your insurance plan or future decisions.

Naming the unknowns is useful. It reminds you that no provider can guarantee a total cost in advance, and it helps you decide which unknowns you are comfortable with.

## Questions About Suitability, Evidence, and Alternatives

Cost is only one part of choosing a provider. You may also want to ask how the provider approaches care, what evidence informs their recommendations, and what alternatives exist. These are questions for a qualified professional, and answers will vary by situation. Examples include:

- How do you decide which tests or approaches to recommend for someone in my situation?
- What does the current evidence say about the options you are considering, including benefits and risks?
- What alternatives should I be aware of, including doing nothing or waiting?
- How will we know whether a plan is helping, and when would we change course?

This article does not evaluate whether any specific treatment is effective or safe, and it does not offer diagnosis or treatment advice. Those conversations belong with a licensed professional who knows your history.

## Practical Tips Before You Decide

- **Get it in writing when possible.** Ask for a written summary of fees, policies, and what is included.
- **Keep a call log.** Note dates, names, and reference numbers for insurance calls.
- **Ask who to contact.** Confirm the best person for billing questions versus clinical questions.
- **Budget for follow-up.** Estimate a reasonable range for the first few months, not just the first visit.
- **Revisit as needed.** If your plan or circumstances change, ask again.

## A Short Note on Next Steps

Before you choose a Functional Medicine Doctor provider, take the time to ask about billing, insurance, and follow-up. The answers will not remove every uncertainty, but they will help you make a more informed decision and reduce the chance of financial surprises. Please discuss your situation with a qualified professional — including your insurer and the provider's billing team — so that the guidance you receive reflects your own circumstances.
